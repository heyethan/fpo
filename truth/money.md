# Money

## Settled

**No equity at the start.** Incubators that claim equity early end up holding something worth
nothing at T+1 or T+2, because there is no moat and no valuation. Incorporation is nine to twelve
months out anyway, and not marrying people to FPO early leaves both sides free to walk.
*Settled 2026-09-17, proposed by Adithya, agreed by Arjun and Ethan.
→ `archive/meetings/meeting-2026-09-17.md`*

**Stipends rather than seed cheques.** In a tier-2 city, doing this in your penultimate year
already cuts against the stigma of getting a job and settling down. A respectable stipend is a
psychological incentive that works better than money a founder may not know how to deploy. Ethan
named the shape: entrepreneur in residence.
*Settled 2026-09-17. → `archive/meetings/meeting-2026-09-17.md`*

**Everyone who contributes gets compensated, eventually.** Ethan's golden rule. The founders are
unpaid right now only because they are the ones starting it, and their time is their investment.
*Stated 2026-09-24. Adithya dissents on timing. See the gap period below.
→ `archive/meetings/meeting-2026-09-24.md`*

**Funds are available and deliberately not deployed.** Ethan has access and will not use it for
the first cohort unless something exceptional appears.
*2026-09-17. → `archive/meetings/meeting-2026-09-17.md`*

## Revenue streams named

1. HR consultancy, placing executives for other companies.
2. General consultancy.
3. Liquidating FPO's interests in the startups it backs.
4. Taking on outside projects.

Adithya's position: the first three are not sufficient for sustenance on their own, for FPO or for
the startups it incubates. The fourth is Arjun's original intake answer, the services company,
arriving as the cash-flow mechanism.

*Named 2026-09-24, not yet chosen between or sequenced.
→ `archive/meetings/meeting-2026-09-24.md`*

## What the market actually pays

Early-stage funds are getting roughly one deal every six months, and that deal competes across
every cohort programme running the same play. A fund will not go past about 10,000 USD at this
stage. The realistic alternative is angels, which incubators already have. The spike comes at
early-to-Series-A.

Ethan's own contacts start at 100,000 USD minimum. A Mumbai contact runs an incubation programme
with roughly 47 companies.

*Adithya's read, 2026-09-17. → `archive/meetings/meeting-2026-09-17.md`*

## The gap period, funded

**Sponsors, brand deals and customers pay for the first cohort.** Nobody funds it out of pocket,
neither the founders nor an investor being burned.

Arjun, stating it: "Fair pay would not mean you get paid your hourly rate, whatever you're
expecting to make a profit. You give a discounted rate to begin with to set up a relationship, even
if it's our plus one who we are bringing into the FPO. So we spend minimally. We cut costs, cut
corners if required, but the money has to come from brand deals and the sponsors and the
customers."

- **Everyone starts at a discounted rate**, including anyone a founder brings in.
- **Research projects pay on success.** Until that money lands, the service projects carry
  everything.
- **Rate cards are needed for outside providers**, not for the four founders.
- **No cut from cohort companies at the start.** Let them reinvest as long as they need. If FPO is
  genuinely funding a company, terms can be set later against milestones, for instance a threshold
  of paying customers before anything comes back.

*Settled 2026-09-30, all four. → `archive/meetings/meeting-2026-09-30.md`*

**Still open:** the formula for redistributing success-based research money when it arrives. Also
Ethan's question from 2026-09-24, accepted as the right one and never answered: how many failures
do we accept as building towards success?

### The dissent this resolved

Adithya had differed from the compensate-everyone rule, seeing a stretch after launch where nobody
or only some people would be paid, with no chosen source of funds. The discounted-rate answer
closed it: everyone is paid less at the start rather than some people not at all.
