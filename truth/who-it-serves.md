# Who it serves

FPO has two sides. The people who come through it, and the people who pay for access to them.

## The talent side

**Scope: Mangalore and coastal Karnataka.** All four founders answered this independently and
converged. No split.

Why this geography, in their own words:

- **Arjun:** enough talent with ease of access, nothing virtual required.
- **Ethan:** tier 2 sits between tier 1 and tier 3, so validating here means "building it in a
  tier 1 city is just a matter of GTM."
- **Adithya:** taught in Mangaluru and at Nitte, holds contacts for legal work and early-stage
  investment, sold property there at 17, Konkani is his first language. "It is the only geography
  where I can call someone and be taken seriously today."

*Settled 2026-09-28, all four. → `archive/exercises/exercise-2-synthesis.md`*

**Who specifically.** The audience is wider than students: graduates, faculty and domain experts
are in scope. The first cohort is drawn from people the founders already know rather than from
open applications.
*From the intake and 2026-09-17. → `archive/meetings/meeting-2026-09-17.md`*

**Who it is not for.** People who want a CV line, who prefer stability to change, who only want a
network, or who want to sell something to the talent. Two founders could not answer this question
at intake.
*From the intake, question B3. → `archive/exercises/intake-synthesis.md`*

## The paying side

This is the least settled part of FPO. Three descriptions exist and have never been put side by
side.

- **Funds and investment bankers.** Adithya's framing: "Essentially, we are a broker." FPO
  surfaces tier-2 startups that never reach LinkedIn or the front page of a newspaper.
- **Companies with problem statements.** Aneesh's framing: businesses pay to have problems solved
  and to reach talent they can trust. The Samsung R&D contact is the example.
- **Would-be entrepreneurs themselves.** Arjun's intake answer, closest to a services model.

*Never reconciled. Carried in `truth/open-questions.md`.
→ `archive/exercises/intake-synthesis.md`*

## Problems come from anywhere

The talent has to be local. The problems do not. Aneesh raised the distinction on 2026-09-28 and
the group settled it two days later.

Arjun: "There is no pond for getting problems fed. It's the ocean. It's infinite. We can get it
from anywhere." He also rejected treating problem suppliers as a separate category at all, calling
it an unnecessary restriction. The two sides are concentric circles rather than two scopes.

**A problem FPO cannot handle** routes to a partner entity, DevVoid, CodexCape, Evlogia, or
Adithya's future law firm, with a portion coming back to FPO and being reinvested. Where FPO can
take it, FPO takes it. Outside projects are welcome regardless, because they build relationships
and give the cohort real work to train on.

*Settled 2026-09-30, all four. → `archive/meetings/meeting-2026-09-30.md`*

**Open:** whether stakeholders filter which problems FPO accepts. If FPO works with an institution,
a municipal body or a government department, do they get a say in what FPO takes on? Adithya raised
it and named the tension: a filter narrows the field, and the cohort is small with limited
expertise.

## Verticals

Narrow to a top three or five rather than staying general. Avoid AI and ML, too crowded to win
partnerships in. Industry 4.0 has untapped avenues. Adithya's filter: no domains the group does
not already know, because entering one without knowledge or curriculum costs too much.
