# Founder intake: agreements and splits

> **Superseded, 2026-09-17.** The founders resolved most of what follows in the meeting that
> night. FPO is commercial, not a nonprofit. The stage is prototyping. The competitor answers were
> altitude rather than disagreement. The problem statement collapsed to one line. What is true now
> lives in `truth/`; this file records what the four of them said before they had talked to each
> other. See `archive/meetings/meeting-2026-09-17.md`.

Four responses: Ethan Kiran Dsouza, Arjun, Adithya Prakash Nayak, Aneesh M Bhat.

## Summary

- **Agree:** FPO should grow people, not act as a cash source or a credential mill. Everyone can give 6 to 8 hours a week or more.
- **Split:** what FPO sells, who pays for it, what stage it is at, and who the competitors are.
- **The gap:** three of four said there is no hidden disagreement. The answers below say there is.

## Theme by theme

### What FPO sells: split
- **Ethan:** a nonprofit that helps students build startups and invests in them, plus training and research.
- **Arjun:** "It does exactly what Infosys does." A services company that takes projects and turns problems it finds into products.
- **Adithya:** a talent pool. Funds and scaling startups come to it for people with a proven record. Mentors consult on contract.
- **Aneesh:** a team that grows ideas. Companies pay to have problem statements solved, and FPO takes a stake in the founders it backs.

### Who pays: split
- **Ethan:** founders, people who want to be founders, and people who think like one.
- **Arjun:** would-be entrepreneurs.
- **Adithya:** funds and scaling companies. Persona: a 39-year-old repeat founder who still doesn't know who he's building for.
- **Aneesh:** decision-makers at companies with problem statements, such as the Samsung R&D contact.

### Stage: split, two against two
- **Building:** Ethan, Adithya.
- **Idea:** Arjun, Aneesh.

### What FPO stands for, in one sentence: partial
All four use the word "enable" or something close to it. They differ on who gets enabled and toward what.
- **Ethan:** "Fostering entrepreneurial and research growth."
- **Arjun:** "Youth enabler."
- **Adithya:** "Finding people with half-formed potential and staying with them until it becomes either a business or a career."
- **Aneesh:** "The central hub where we work together to enable students, enable companies, and enable all the entities involved."

### The problem FPO solves: split
- **Ethan:** Mangalore is a tier-2 city. Research pays badly and opportunities are few, so talent leaves.
- **Arjun:** people with market-validated ideas have no way to build an MVP or launch.
- **Adithya:** programmes teach founders to build a project, not a business. Nobody tells them early that there's no business there.
- **Aneesh:** companies with problem statements need talent they can trust.

### Known for and against: partial
- **Ethan:** for growth and the right support. Against being a place to get money, or a credit-point scheme for colleges.
- **Arjun:** for enabling youth, technically and personally. Against being a cult.
- **Adithya:** for telling founders early and honestly whether there's a business, and for mentorship that lasts past the cohort. Against certificate programmes posing as incubation, and judging success by applicant numbers.
- **Aneesh:** for talent and real problem-solving, a frontier innovation hub. Against being a money-first investor.

Three of four reject the money-or-credentials version of FPO. Arjun's worry is a different one.

### Competitors: split, no overlap
- **Ethan:** Vizuora.
- **Arjun:** Cosh, DT Labz.
- **Adithya:** InUnity, DK24, TiE Mangaluru, CEOL, KDEM.
- **Aneesh:** KTECH, IIC. Doesn't see them as real competition.

### Who FPO is not for: gaps
- **Ethan:** couldn't answer.
- **Arjun:** nothing came to mind.
- **Adithya:** people who want a CV line and prefer stability to change.
- **Aneesh:** people who only want a network, or who want to sell to the talent.

### Hours per week: agree
Ethan: as needed. Arjun: 8. Adithya: 6 to 8, more if needed. Aneesh: 7 or more.

### Why each founder is here
- **Ethan:** to help Mangalore grow and give tier-2 students the chances Ethan didn't get.
- **Arjun:** a strong student workforce, market connections, fame.
- **Adithya:** to build one entity that reflects well on the region and the country. Doesn't need credit.
- **Aneesh:** to grow businesses, work on problems with talented people, and eventually do R&D.

### The two-column contrarian exercise
Only Adithya wrote it as rows ("they say / I say"), four of them. The other three wrote a general complaint:
- **Ethan:** government schemes are badly run, and curricula are out of date.
- **Arjun:** programmes keep "the cream" rather than drawing talent out of everyone.
- **Aneesh:** enablers hand out opportunities instead of teaching hands-on.

4. **Contrarian beliefs:** three founders still need to write theirs as rows before exercise one.
