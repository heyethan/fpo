# Exercise Two — Credibility and Scope: what the four answers show

**Source exercise:** Ralston, *Disgustingly Good Personal Brand* ch2 (pond/lake/ocean) and ch3
(expert vs student). Questions in `brand/exercise-2-credibility.md`.
**Responses:** all four. Arjun 09-24, Adithya 09-26, Aneesh and Ethan 09-28.

Candidate splits below are labelled **worth testing in the room** rather than reported as findings.
A restatement test run by one reader is a hypothesis; four founders restating to each other is the
test. Exercise one taught us that the hard way.

---

## The headline: selectivity

Q2 has failed twice — in the written round, and again on 2026-09-24 where it was skipped. This time
three of four answers converge on the same mechanism.

**Adithya moved off his own earlier position and made the argument:**

> "Reliability has to be awarded by someone else, so we cannot claim it yet. Selectivity is
> something we do, starting with the first person we turn down. An incubator running forty a
> cohort cannot say no, and once it cannot say no, its word on anyone inside is worth nothing. I
> side with Arjun here."

This is the strongest line the workshop has produced, for three reasons. It is an action FPO takes
rather than a quality FPO claims. It explains why selectivity produces the thing everyone actually
wants, an FPO endorsement that carries weight. And it answers Arjun's InUnity question from
2026-09-17 without having been asked: an incubator that cannot say no cannot vouch.

**Aneesh independently describes the same mechanism:**

> "FPO is a tight knit group for sure, and also known for reliability as well. The main thing is
> small group who we can reliably grow to good heights... ensure that the team is solid and the
> idea is solid and the foundations are solid."

**Arjun:** "Passion>Possession." A value rather than a reputation, and on a different axis from the
other three. He also named this question as the hardest, "because I cant find the differentiator as
easily."

**Ethan:** "raise talent through the different facets of how we enable that process." Still at the
altitude that failed the previous two attempts, and he said so directly: "many things come to my
mind. I can't solidify them into one strong point that I can carry across every single time."

**Worth testing in the room:** whether selectivity is the reputation itself, or the mechanism that
earns one. Adithya's own phrasing points at the second reading.

## FPO is a student. Its founders mostly are not.

Asked what FPO itself could point at today, nobody claimed a track record.

- **Arjun:** "Nothing? Not even registered yet..."
- **Adithya:** "Brainstorming, networking, floating the idea to stakeholders, mapping who else
  operates here. No cohort, no pilot, no revenue, nobody placed. The four of us bring track record."
- **Aneesh:** "it has done tonnes of things regarding prep... for me that still feels like nothing
  yet, even tho the foundation is somewhat laid."
- **Ethan:** the only one counting anything — an upcoming pilot with St Aloysius, and eight months
  of R&D talking to stakeholders.

The personal credibility, by contrast, is real and specific: tech research and student training and
a digital services company (Arjun); the startup ecosystem since 2021, failures and successes and
helping others incorporate (Ethan); founding-team failures, CTO mistakes including production
incidents, compliance and operations (Aneesh); and Adithya's full ledger, from Chronos in 2017
through Farak's two 10,000-plus-attendee conferences, Effinity, Rapture's INR 91 lakh Indiegogo
raise, teaching 450-plus CA and CS students, PE and VC internships, and SPV and trust structuring
in ADGM.

**The constraint this creates:** the founders can make expert claims. The entity cannot. Anything
FPO says about itself this year has to be backed by what the four of them have done, because FPO
has not done anything yet. That is a student brand with expert operators inside it, and Ralston has
no page for that combination.

## Expert or student

| Founder | Position | In their words |
|---|---|---|
| Arjun | Expert | "I know my roadmap, I can generalise it for students who will walk it." |
| Aneesh | Expert by the definition given | "the word expert doesn't feel right to me, but from the definition of the expert used here yes i can teach and make sure to grow the cohort." |
| Ethan | Between | "I am not a student, but I am not an expert either, because the bar for what I consider to be an expert is high." |
| Adithya | Student | "I have started four things and none of them is still trading. I know every mistake in the first eighteen months because I have made most of them." |

Ethan and Aneesh refused the word for the same stated reason, a high personal bar, while describing
themselves as meeting the definition given on the form. Recorded as vocabulary rather than a
difference. Both read as expert.

Nobody postured as an expert, which is Ralston's one sin. Adithya's answer is the most valuable in
the set because it does the opposite:

> "I start things well and I have not yet carried one past the point where enthusiasm runs out.
> That is the exact failure FPO is meant to solve for other people, which is either the best reason
> to trust me on it or the worst."

He named this question as his hardest: "Writing out four ventures that no longer exist, in a row,
is not how I usually present myself."

**Worth testing in the room:** Ralston offers no way to combine four positions into an entity-level
one. The evidence points at FPO being a student entity regardless of how many experts sit inside
it, because the entity's own bank is empty.

## Pond: converged, and possibly two

All four put the **cohort** side in Mangalore and coastal Karnataka. The split predicted before this
round did not appear.

- **Arjun:** "Pond is DK because you'll find enough talent with ease of access, nothing virtual
  required."
- **Ethan:** Mangalore and coastal Karnataka. Tier 2 sits between tier 1 and tier 3, so validating
  here means "building it in a tier 1 city is just a matter of GTM."
- **Adithya:** the sharpest justification, and exactly the test Ralston sets. He taught in Mangaluru
  and at Nitte, holds contacts for legal work and early-stage investment, sold property there at
  17, and Konkani is his first language. "It is the only geography where I can call someone and be
  taken seriously today."

**Aneesh drew a distinction nobody else made:**

> "if the pond is talking about the students then its karnataka mainly mangalore as the starting
> point. other than that regarding the connectors i.e. people who give problem statements and all
> that i do think can be international."

FPO has two sides, so it may have two ponds: a local talent pond, and a supply-of-problems side
that is not geographically bound. Ralston's chapter assumes one person and one scope.

**Worth testing in the room:** whether the connector side is genuinely unbounded, or whether FPO's
standing to approach an international problem-giver is itself set by the local track record it does
not have yet.

## Where this leaves the workshop

**Settled enough to build on:** the pond is Mangalore and coastal Karnataka. FPO as an entity has no
track record and must borrow its founders'. Nobody is posturing.

**The live candidate:** selectivity, as the thing FPO is known for. Three of four founders behind it
in substance, with a mechanism and an argument for why it beats the alternative.

**Still open:** whether selectivity is the reputation or the means to it; whether the connector side
has its own pond; how four expert-student positions combine into one for the entity; and the
gap-period funding question carried from 2026-09-24, untouched by this round.

**Next exercise:** the contrarian two-column. Selectivity gives it a spine — the left column has an
obvious first row, which is what the rest of the space believes about cohort size and access.
