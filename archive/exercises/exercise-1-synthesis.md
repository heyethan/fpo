> **Superseded in part, 2026-09-24.** The founders reviewed this together and collapsed the
> people-vs-revenue split below as vocabulary: all four want FPO to profit, and Adithya simply made
> FPO the subject of the sentence. Research was settled as a side effect. The wins-vs-failures
> publishing split dissolved into "record everything, filter by channel." Q2 failed a second time
> and was skipped. See `brand/meeting-2026-09-24.md`. What follows stands as a record of what the

**Source exercise:** Ralston, *Disgustingly Good Personal Brand* ch1 / *How to Start a Personal
Brand* ch2. Questions in `brand/exercise-1-journey.md`.
**Responses:** all four, 2026-09-20 to 2026-09-23.

Every claim below was put through the restatement test: an answer counts as a split only if it
survives being restated in another founder's words. Where it collapses, it is recorded as
vocabulary and merged. Original answers are kept alongside so any merge can be overruled.

## Q1 — Desired outcome

### Merged (three of four say the same thing)

A small, countable group of people comes through FPO within three years, and a measurable share of
them succeed as founders or as placed talent.

- **Arjun:** "at least 10 greatful students who were able to be more than they were using us as an
  instrument."
- **Adithya:** 20 people through; ≥5 running companies "still trading on customer revenue rather
  than grants or seed funding"; ≥3 placed in senior roles or fund-backed on FPO's assessment.
- **Aneesh:** founders with traction plus R&D in place; one good cohort a year, cohorts 1–3 staged
  by year three.

These differ in precision, not direction. 10 / 20 / "a cohort a year" are compatible.

**Ethan sits one level up, not in a different direction.** "For Mangalore to be known as a Tier 1
city through our efforts... social innovation and social impact is the end goal." That is the
consequence of the other three outcomes, not a rival to them. Recorded as altitude.

### Survives restatement — two real differences

**1. Whether FPO getting paid is part of winning.** Only Adithya's outcome contains it: *"Money has
to have changed hands in both directions: FPO has been paid at least 10-12x by a fund or a company,
not only by the people it mentors."* No other founder's definition of success includes FPO earning
anything. This is the intake's unresolved who-pays question, now visible in what each founder
counts as a win.

**2. Whether research is part of the outcome.** Aneesh requires R&D by year three, specifically on
the software side. Arjun wants at least one outsourced research problem delivered to spec. Ethan
lists "fostering research." Adithya's outcome contains no research at all.

## Q2 — What FPO must be known for

**The weak round.** Three of four named a category of activity rather than a reputation:

- **Ethan:** "works with startups and founders and basically talent, helping them grow"
- **Aneesh:** "a place that facilitates and supports talent... structure things, move things in the
  right manner, and keep iterating"
- **Arjun:** "Quality resources, tinkering mindset."

Those three collapse into one another under restatement. None of them distinguishes FPO from
InUnity, DK24 or any other incubator, which is Arjun's differentiation question from 2026-09-17
arriving from a different direction.

**Adithya is the exception:** *"for being reliable"* — the only answer naming a reputation rather
than an activity, and the only one with a Q3 that says how it gets earned.

Ralston's Q2 asks for the specific reputation that produces the Q1 outcome. On that test, this
round has one answer and three placeholders. **Ethan named Q2 as the hardest question to write.**

---

## Q3 — What FPO must do

### Convergence: publishing is the mechanism

Ethan, Adithya and Aneesh independently land on visibility and publishing as how the reputation
gets earned. Worth noting against the 2026-09-17 decision to stay quiet for three months: the
disagreement there is about timing, not about whether.

- **Ethan:** partner with institutions, work with corporations, support the community, "a certain
  kind of marketing to maintain our reputation and also compound our growth socially."
- **Aneesh:** a personal brand for each founder, or at least a shared platform, "not in the sense
  of publicising, but rather to showcase our capabilities."
- **Adithya:** one piece a month in his own name, within the 6–8 hours he committed.

### Survives restatement: a split inside the shared word

**What gets published is opposite.**

- **Aneesh publishes wins:** "we really just publicise them, do PR around them, and all that good
  stuff," focused on the initial cohort.
- **Adithya publishes failures:** outcomes at 12, 24 and 36 months "including the ones that failed
  and the people who left"; predictions written up beforehand and checked against what happened;
  people turned down visibly and told why, because "a list that everyone gets onto is worth
  nothing."

Same vocabulary, opposite content strategy. This is the inverse of the round-1 pattern: there the
wording differed and the meaning matched, here the wording matches and the meaning differs.

Adithya's answer is also the only one carrying an explicit differentiation claim: *"Nobody in this
space does this, which is precisely why doing it is worth something."*

**Arjun's Q3 is a set of targets rather than actions:** "Result driven, have 1 successful
team/student per batch, have at least one outsourced research problem whose DOR and DOD are met by
us." Closer to Q1 material than Q3.

## Q4 — Gaps

- **Adithya:** can't yet describe how to evidence a person's judgement so an outsider accepts it,
  "which is the whole business." And: *"I am not sure of the sequence of funds injected into the FPO
  because my previous response includes funds, companies and mentor consulting in the same breath,
  which means I have not chosen."*
- **Aneesh:** confident end-to-end on software and on business taste. Gaps: structuring for
  investment (states a bootstrapping preference), compliance and tax, and brand positioning.
- **Arjun:** how to get research problems opened to FPO, and how to motivate students toward
  questions whose answers nobody has.
- **Ethan:** "the synergy where everything comes together at one point. That is something I still
  don't have clarity about."

Adithya and Aneesh between them name the same missing piece from two sides: nobody has chosen
where the money comes from, and nobody knows how to make FPO's judgement of a person legible to an
outsider. Those two are the same problem — the second is what would make the first sellable.

## Where this leaves the workshop

**Not a split, despite appearances:** the Q1 outcomes, and the Q2 answers from Ethan, Arjun and
Aneesh. Different precision and different altitude, same direction.

**Genuine, and unresolved:**

1. **What counts as FPO succeeding — people improved, or a business that gets paid.** Only one
   founder's definition of success includes revenue to FPO. This is the same question left open on
   2026-09-17 and it now has to be answered before Q2 can be.
2. **Publish wins or publish failures.** Two opposite strategies under one word.
3. **Is research part of the outcome or a side effect.**

**The blocker for the next exercise:** Q2 has one real answer and three placeholders. A reputation
that doesn't separate FPO from InUnity can't produce a contrarian position, and the contrarian rows
are the next step. Re-running Q2 against a settled Q1 is likely to be worth more than pushing
forward.

**Resolution warning for any map drawn from this:** Adithya answered in falsifiable thresholds; the
other three answered directionally. A visual will render that as disagreement when it is a
difference in how the question was taken. Flag it on the map rather than letting the shapes imply it.
