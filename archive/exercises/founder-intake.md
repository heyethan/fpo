# FPO — Founder Intake (Round 1: Context)

**For:** all four founders, answered **independently**. Do not discuss with each other first.
**Time:** 25–35 minutes. Written, not verbal.

**Three rules before you start:**
1. **Answer alone.** If you sync up first, I get one answer four times and learn nothing.
2. **Be concrete, not impressive.** "We help teams move faster" is word-shaped air. "We cut the time a
   dispatcher spends reconciling a delivery exception from 40 minutes to 4" is a fact. If someone
   couldn't draw it or check it, rewrite it.
3. **Where you don't know, write "I don't know."** A confident guess is worse than a blank.

---

## Section 0 — Who's answering

**Q0.** Your name.

---

## Section A — The Thing (5 questions)

**A1.** In two sentences max, what does FPO do? Write it as if explaining to a smart friend who
doesn't work in this industry.

**A2.** What does FPO sell, or plan to sell, and who hands over money for it? (If this isn't
decided, say what you'd sell if forced to pick today.)

**A3.** Finish this sentence with something checkable: *"Before FPO, the way this gets done today is
____, and that costs the customer ____."* (Cost in hours, money, stress, or reputation.)

**A4.** What stage do you think FPO is actually at right now? Pick one and say why in one line:
idea / building / early users / early revenue / existing work getting a name.

**A5.** What has FPO actually done so far that you could point at as proof? List anything real —
prototypes, pilots, conversations, revenue, prior work by the four of you that counts. If the honest
answer is "nothing yet," write that.

---

## Section B — Who It's For (3 questions)

**B1.** Describe the single narrowest customer FPO is genuinely for. Not the market — one person.
Their role, their company or situation, what their Tuesday looks like.

**B2.** What is the most **painful** problem that person has that FPO addresses? Painful = expensive
to keep living with. Say what it costs them.

**B3.** Who is FPO explicitly **not** for? Name a type of customer you'd turn down.

---

## Section C — The Landscape (2 questions)

**C1.** Name up to three competitors or alternatives — including "they do nothing / use a
spreadsheet" if that's the real alternative. For each, one line on what they do well.

**C2.** What do people in this space say, or do, that you fundamentally disagree with? Write as many
as you genuinely hold. Not "what would sound edgy" — things you'd argue about at dinner. For each,
write what you believe instead.

> Two columns. Left: what they say. Right: what you'd say instead. Minimum three rows if you can.

---

## Section D — You, Personally (4 questions)

**D1.** Why are *you* doing this? Not the deck answer. What do you personally want out of FPO in
three years?

**D2.** In your own words: what does FPO stand for? One sentence. Don't coordinate — I want the four
versions side by side.

**D3.** Name two things you want FPO to be known **for**, and two things you want it to be known
**against**.

**D4.** What's your personal credibility here — what have you actually done that earns you the right
to work on this? Include the failures and what they taught you; those count as much as the wins.

---

## Section E — Constraints & Non-Negotiables (3 questions)

**E1.** Is there anything already fixed that a brand has to live with? (An existing name in use, a
domain owned, a customer who knows you as something, a legal or regulatory constraint.)

**E2.** Realistically, how much time per week can *you* give to brand, content, and being publicly
visible for FPO? Be honest — this number shapes everything downstream.

**E3.** Is there anything you'd refuse to do for this brand? (Be on camera, take a strong public
stance, work with certain partners, a tone you won't use.)

---

## Section F — One Freeform (1 question)

**F1.** Anything you believe about FPO that you suspect the other three don't agree with? This is the
most useful box on the form. Say it here rather than in the room.

---

*Send answers back as-is, all four sets, unedited. I want to see the disagreements, not a merged
document.*
