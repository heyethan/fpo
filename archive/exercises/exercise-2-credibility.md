# FPO — Exercise Two: Credibility, Scope, and What FPO Is Known For

**Source:** Ralston, *Disgustingly Good Personal Brand* ch2 (Pick Your Pond, Not the Ocean) and
ch3 (Earn the Right to Be Heard: Expert vs Student).

**For:** all four founders, answered independently. Do not discuss first.
**Time:** 25 to 35 minutes.

**Adaptation note.** Both chapters are written for one person building a personal brand. FPO is
four people building a company, so each of you answers for yourself first, then for FPO. Where
the four answers differ, that is the material for the next session, not a problem to smooth over
before submitting.

**Answer in the order given.** The last question depends on the ones before it. It is the same
question that got skipped on the 24th, and it gets a better answer once your receipts are written
down.

---

**Q1.** What have *you* actually done that earns you the right to work on this? List the wins and
the failures, and put the lesson next to each one.

> Ralston: "the success of your personal brand is predicated on what you've actually done." The
> failures count as much as the wins. A failure with a lesson attached is a receipt. A win with
> nothing learned from it is a line on a CV.

**Q2.** On this specific subject, are you an **expert** or a **student**, honestly?

> Expert means you have already done the thing our cohort wants to do. Student means you are on
> the same road, a few steps ahead. Both are legitimate. The only losing move is a student
> posturing as an expert, and Ralston is blunt that audiences smell it.
>
> Say which one you are and why, in a line or two.

**Q3.** What has **FPO itself** done so far that it could point at today? Anything real: pilots,
conversations, prior work by the four of you that counts, people already helped. If the honest
answer is nothing yet, write that.

**Q4.** What is FPO's **pond**? Pick one and say why in a line.

> - **Pond** — Mangalore and coastal Karnataka
> - **Lake** — Karnataka or south India
> - **Ocean** — India-wide
>
> The test is track record, not ambition. Ralston: "your credibility ceiling is set by your track
> record, not your opinions." Picking a scope wider than the evidence means competing against
> people who have actually done it there, with nothing to add. The move up is earned later.

**Q5.** Given everything above: what must FPO be **known for**?

> One reputation, not a list. It has to be something FPO can back with your answers to Q1 and Q3,
> and something an incubator running 40 students a cohort could not say about itself.
>
> Two answers surfaced when we discussed this on the 24th and neither got written down. Adithya:
> reliability, with the objection that you cannot own reliability unless somebody awards it to
> you. Arjun: a tight-knit group that does not take everyone, where the people inside are in safe
> hands. Use them, argue with them, or ignore them.
