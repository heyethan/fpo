## Two corrections to the synthesis and map

**1. The people-vs-revenue split does not exist.** It was the one difference the synthesis said
survived the restatement test, and the map put it on the horizontal axis as the real split. The
room collapsed it.

- Arjun: "mine could also be revenue because out of those 10 students, I've written that at least
  one of them does make a successful career out of it, which is kind of revenue only."
- Ethan: cohort-by-cohort revenue was in his answer too.
- Arjun: "we are more or less aligned, bro. We all want profit. Adithya probably worded it more
  money-oriented."
- Ethan's reading, accepted by the group: Aneesh and Arjun phrased success as students benefiting;
  Adithya phrased it as FPO benefiting as well. Same intent, different subject of the sentence.

Method note: the restatement test was run on the written answers by Claude alone and it passed.
Four founders restating to each other is the stronger test. Treat the written version as a
hypothesis, not a finding.

**2. "Falsifiable" is a failed label.** Ethan, in the room: "one thing I don't understand is
falsifiable. Like, anyone make sense out of that direction?" Nobody could. The word came from
Harry Dry's three rules; it did no work for the people reading the map. The vertical axis needs
replacing, and with the horizontal axis gone too, the map needs redrawing rather than relabelling.

## What the group settled

**Their own framing for the divergence: elevation.** Ethan: "our visions are aligned regardless.
It is the elevation. Like, we are all looking at it in different planes." He placed himself at the
end goal, ignoring what sits underneath; Adithya at the long term with a plan and focused KPIs;
Arjun at the long-term vision while sizing what the success metric looks like.

Arjun set the test for whether that matters: "It's not conflicting thoughts... nothing is a problem
until it's conflicting. If there's something that I want to do that we cannot do while Adithya's
statement also holds true, then it's not a problem. It's just a question of are we accepting each
other's terms. Are we red flagging anybody else's optics." Conclusion: a wider range of
definitions is fine.

**Research is a side effect, not an outcome.** Arjun: "It's a tree. It's a branch." No dissent.
Closes one of the three open questions from the synthesis.

**Compensation, as a principle.** Ethan: anyone involved in FPO at any level who contributes
towards it needs to be compensated. That is the golden rule. Founders are unpaid right now only
because they are the ones starting it, and their time is their investment.

**Publishing: record everything, filter by channel.** Dissolves the wins-vs-failures split the
synthesis drew.

- Ethan: record every single thing, inception to execution.
- Adithya: the story framing. Startups get covered at launch and then nobody knows what happened;
  it takes someone investigating years later. Publish the whole arc, including the incubator
  itself, not just the launch.
- Ethan proposed separate accounts by content type, with a low-frequency portfolio account for
  startups. Arjun pushed back on the maintenance burden ("won't it be like you're maintaining
  three, four channels... that becomes a full-time job"). Ethan: filtration, not extra recording,
  and a portfolio account posts rarely.
- Arjun aligned. Ethan flagged it as a conversation for later.

## What is still open

### The gap period — the real version of the who-pays question

Everyone agrees FPO should earn. Nobody has decided what happens between incubating the first
startup and anyone being paid.

**Adithya dissented from the golden rule as stated:** "I slightly differ on compensating every
single person who is part of the FPU." He sees a stretch after launch where nobody, or only some
people, get paid, and says the source of funds for that stretch is unchosen — contributions, their
own pockets, or something else. He proposed putting metrics or stages on when people start getting
paid rather than promising it flatly.

**Arjun sharpened it:** if the money comes from their pockets or from investors, and students
succeed but no cash flows back — a placement, an open-source project, a startup that helps someone
without returning money — does that count as success, or as a story building towards success?

**Ethan's counter-question, which the group accepted as the right one and did not answer:** how
many of these failures are we going to accept as building towards success?

**Ethan's three cases for unpaid contributors, also unresolved:** tell them to wait for payment
later; work free in exchange for certification; or go to an investor up front.

**Adithya's refinement:** it depends on the stakeholder's seniority. A junior contributor can take
the wait-and-be-paid model. Someone senior, already compensated elsewhere and treating this as a
side project, needs another consideration — for instance a right vested in them to acquire shares
in a startup of their choice.

**Cash-flow streams named:** HR consultancy placing executives for other companies; general
consultancy; liquidating FPO's interests in incubated startups; and taking on outside projects.
Ethan recalled the last one as a decision already made in an earlier meeting. Adithya's position is
that the first three are not sufficient for sustenance on their own, for FPO or for the startups.

Note: taking on outside projects is Arjun's round-one intake answer — the services company — now
arriving as the cash-flow mechanism.

### Q2, what FPO must be known for — failed a second time, explicitly skipped

Ethan: "let's skip this question for now."

The word "category" derailed it. It came from the map's card text ("named a category of activity
rather than a reputation"), was read as the operative term, and the group spent the segment asking
what a category is — a school, a business, a service company. Ethan offered examples: acceleration,
research incubation, training, fundraising.

Two real answers surfaced anyway:

- **Adithya:** reliability. Whoever works with FPO — funds, companies looking for good heads,
  anyone in a consultancy capacity — gets a higher degree of reliability and "not just the puffery
  that most of our competitors carry out." His own objection: reliability cannot be owned as a
  category, because it would take a third party giving an award, which is not what FPO is going for.
- **Arjun:** a tight-knit group that does not take everyone, where the people inside "are in safe
  hands... we are all aligned with them and they are aligned with us to grow together."

Arjun's is closer to a reputation than anything in the written round. Neither was recorded as an
answer during the call.
